# Global rational B-spline meridian proposal

Status: source-only minimum-jerk candidate driver is ready for review. No basis extraction, Hessian assembly, KKT solve, control net, curve sample, offset, CAD shape, or solver result has been computed.

## Why replace the Hermite chain

The earlier septic chain forced second and third derivatives to zero at every shared station. At a regular station that forces signed curvature to zero, creating repeated curvature resets even when positions and tangents join smoothly. Its short 4/8 mm base parameter spans also concentrated turning and made a foot shelf visually likely. The jar shoulder produced a finite point counterexample to the retained 9 mm body-radius screen. Root's exact review of the prior candidate recorded 4 of 12 target spans passing and 8 failing; this proposal leaves that history unchanged.

The new representation has one degree-5 clamped rational B-spline for the complete outside meridian, from the axis-bottom to the outer mouth. Its five internal knots are simple, so the curve is C4 there. Position interpolation at a feature does not constrain its second derivative. Only the fixed maximum-radius apex is given a vertical tangent and strict radial-maximum condition; curvature at other knots is free to be nonzero. The internal knots are parameter breaks, not repeated legacy stations or little independently tuned corner patches.

## Fixed data and control variables

Each family uses eleven unknown RZ control points `Q0…Q10`, with fixed positive rational unit weights. The clamped degree-5 knot vector is `(0×6, 1/6, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 5/6, 1×6)`. Unit weights keep point and tangent constraints linear in the 22 scalar control coordinates. Changing weights would make those constraints rational/nonlinear and requires a new review.

The only interpolated geometric features are axis-bottom, maximum-radius apex, and outer mouth. Bowl preserves `(0,0)`, `(60,77.4)`, and `(57,90)`; jar preserves `(0,0)`, `(54,62)`, and `(26,120)`. The clear mouth diameters remain 108 and 46 mm. The mouth/body datums stay 90/120 mm. The accepted candidate total crest heights are 92/122 mm; the 2 mm rise belongs to the future lip/crest design and is not checked by this body-only source. No old base-turn height is fixed. In particular the prior z=6/5.5 outer-turn stations are not equated to the catalog base-thickness declaration; that declaration is reserved for later inner-floor design. The old jar `(54,40.8)` plateau station is not imposed.

For each case, `bspline_constraint_source.py` can assemble exact rational rows without a linear algebra dependency:

- 6 scalar feature-position equalities (three RZ points);
- 3 scalar tangent equalities: zero vertical derivative at the axis, zero radial derivative at the mouth, and zero radial derivative at the apex.
- Strict linear direction inequalities: outward radial axis tangent, upward mouth/apex tangents, and negative radial second derivative at the apex. The latter makes the fixed `Rmax` a strict local radial maximum rather than a platform.

These are 9 equalities in 22 scalar controls before inequalities. The new fixed candidate rule removes the arbitrary free-control choice: minimize the exact rational fairness functional `∫||C'''(u)||²du` on the unit parameter domain, independently for `r` and `z`, subject to the nine declared equalities. This is a deterministic mathematical candidate, not a style fit or material calibration. The driver uses four small exact KKT systems total (r and z for bowl, then r and z for jar): dimensions 16 and 15 per family. It checks equality-row rank exactly and fails closed on a singular KKT system; it uses no ridge, pseudoinverse, floating solve, guessed controls, or optimization package.

The Hessian is assembled analytically from the exact Boehm-extracted degree-5 Bezier spans. On a span of parameter width `h`, the third derivative is `60/h³` times the degree-2 Bernstein polynomial of third forward differences, so the exact Gram contribution is `3600/h⁵` times the degree-2 Bernstein Gram matrix. The nullspace of this fairness Hessian consists of componentwise global quadratics: zero integral forces each local third derivative to vanish, and C4 joins make the local quadratics one global quadratic. For either coordinate, the three fixed feature values at distinct `u` values annihilate every homogeneous global quadratic. Thus the Hessian is positive definite on the equality nullspace; with full equality-row rank this gives a nonsingular KKT system. The runtime exact rank and solve checks still fail closed instead of relying on this proof alone.

## Shape and regularity constraints for a later review

Exact Boehm insertion raises each of the five simple knots to multiplicity five and yields six degree-5 Bezier spans, each with poles that are exact linear combinations of the 11 B-spline poles. The driver compares endpoint jets through order four at every span join, checking C4 directly. It checks control-hull bounds `0≤r≤Rmax` and `0≤z≤mouth_z`, nonnegative vertical derivative poles with at least one positive pole per open span, radial derivative sign before/after the apex, and every strict linear tangent/apex inequality. These are sufficient shape and regularity screens. The derivative-pole test proves `z′>0` on each open span; endpoint tangent vectors are checked separately. A mixed-sign derivative-pole vector or out-of-bounds pole means the sufficient certificate was not obtained; it does not by itself prove the curve is nonmonotone or outside bounds. The checks do not impose zero curvature at internal knots.

The body/shoulder minimum-radius screen remains fixed at 9 mm. The separate outer base-turn screens are 9 mm for bowl and 8.25 mm for jar (1.5 times their catalog base-thickness declarations), and are geometry screens only. For a regular profile parameter `u`, define `S=(r′)^2+(z′)^2` and `D=r′z″−z′r″`. On a region whose fixed radius target is `R`, the continuum condition is `S^3−R^2D^2≥0` together with `S>0`. The driver constructs the exact degree-24 Bernstein coefficients of `P_R=S^3−R²D²` for each full span, and reports both radius targets on all spans because the base-turn interval is not assigned. Nonnegative coefficients suffice to certify `P_R≥0`; mixed-sign coefficients remain UNKNOWN unless an endpoint value is negative. This is not a region-specific base certificate, and no sampled minimum substitutes for it.

There is a genuine geometry feasibility risk near the base. Under a monotone-height, no-turn-back profile, rotating the tangent from horizontal to vertical with curvature magnitude at most `1/R` requires a vertical rise of at least `R`: `dz=sin(θ)ds`, and `|dθ/ds|≤1/R`. Thus an old vertical base join at z=6 mm (bowl) or 5.5 mm (jar) cannot meet radii 9 and 8.25 mm respectively under those assumptions. Those failed outer turn heights are intentionally free here; the base-thickness numbers are not used as outer-turn targets. If the constrained spline needs an outer foot rise that is visually or dimensionally unacceptable, the geometry and retained screen conflict and root must choose the design tradeoff. The 9 mm target must not be relaxed to force a pass.

For a later 3 mm inward normal offset, the exact local signed condition is `1−3κ(u)>0` at every point. A proved `|κ|≤1/R` with `R≥9` implies this condition with margin, but it is only sufficient: the prior jar-shoulder screen counterexample had negative signed curvature around `−0.35874 mm⁻¹`, so `1−3κ≈2.076>0`; that absolute-radius failure did not establish an offset cusp. Any local signed-factor failure is a separate point/span rejection. Global offset reach, self-intersection, and minimum material interval remain UNKNOWN until separately bounded; the catalog's 3 mm radial declaration is not actual normal wall thickness.

The axis endpoint is regular as a meridian when `dr/du>0` and `dz/du=0`; the resulting surface has a horizontal tangent plane at the axis. The outer mouth requires `dr/du=0,dz/du>0`. At the apex the same upward vertical tangent plus `r″<0` gives a smooth local radius maximum. The unit-weight spline is C4 at simple internal knots and imposes no zero-curvature condition there.
